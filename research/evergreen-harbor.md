# Evergreen Harbor: how the game ships it, and what her plan changes (15 Oct)

For the Evergreen Harbor community-lot discussion (TODO §5). Sources: the Sims Wiki (Evergreen Harbor, Community space, Small business pages), EA forum player reports, and the game's own lot descriptions (research/data/game-lots.json).

## As the game ships it

- **13 lots:** 7 homes, 2 apartment buildings (4 units), and 4 community lots.
- **The 4 community lots:**
  - 3 community spaces, all 40×30, one per neighbourhood: The Quarry Building (Grims Quarry), Sprucewood Square (Conifer Station) and The Waterfront (Port Promise).
  - The Caboose, a bar.
- **No shops, café or restaurant at all.**
- **Families:**
  - Harris: Miner Mansion.
  - Sterling Rico: The Old Mill. An entrepreneurial "power couple"; Bess is a civil designer, Jules a style influencer.
  - Greenburg: The Portsmouth Promenade. Mary gardens, Blossom paints up unused places, and their son Knox is an activist.
  - Tinker: Pigulock Manor. Tina is a freelance crafter, Yasemin her wife, Olive their daughter.
- **Empty homes:** Canal Corner, The Shipping Views and Rockridge Springs.
- **Eco footprint:** Grims Quarry starts green, Conifer Station neutral, Port Promise industrial.

## Her plan against that

- **Three family homes become small businesses** run by the game's own families:
  - The Old Mill: Sterling Rico (they live in Stonestreet #4).
  - The Portsmouth Promenade: the Greenburgs (Knox lives in Stonestreet #3). Built and shared as a community business with classes and a port clean-up.
  - Canal Corner: the Tinkers.
- **Port Promise's community space moves.** The Waterfront becomes the Abandoned Views rental, and the community space goes on The Shipping Views (The Old Dockyard). All four versions are built and shared.
- **Same number of community spaces as the game (3).** The Quarry Building is ticked as built but isn't shared; Sprucewood Square isn't built.

## Community spaces (Sims Wiki)

- They can go in any non-destination world.
- **One lot, four versions,** each with its own build and lot traits. Neighbours in that neighbourhood vote weekly at the voting board to pick the active version:
  - Community space: needs only the voting board.
  - Community garden: 4 planters, toilet, sink and bin; optionally insect farms and vertical gardens.
  - Marketplace: 3 sales tables, toilet, sink and bin; optionally market stalls and picnic tables.
  - Maker space: fabricator, recycler, toilet, sink and bin; optionally woodworking, easel, candle making, juice fizzer and a kids' activity.
- **So each community space is four builds.** That's why they feel like a lot of space.
- **Player reports (EA forums; may be out of date):**
  - Maker spaces get the most visitors.
  - Marketplaces get only 1–3 vendors, and food stalls mostly stay empty (only the Eco stall, bars, sales tables and the street gallery get staffed).

## Small businesses (Sims Wiki)

- **A café works.** Nordhaven's Koffieboon is a small business: coffee, tea, reading and classes.
- **Proper meals are awkward.** There are no waiters, food is self-serve from a counter, and the owner must be on the lot.
- **They open when their owner opens them.** Player reports on NPC-owned ones opening by themselves are mixed. Café, bar and restaurant lot types run on their own.
